# Gentle Path — competitive & market research brief

## Objective

Inform what "elite tier" should mean for Gentle Path across three things: standing out, marketing right, and being very good all around. This is research to inform decisions Claude and Suraj will make together — not a request to propose features or pick a direction.

## Read first

`docs/product/PRODUCT_THESIS.md` and `docs/product/MVP.md` (or `docs/PRODUCT_THESIS.md` / `docs/MVP.md` if the product/ copies still don't exist — check both). These define what this app is and deliberately is not. Nothing in this research should be read as "recommend adding this" if it conflicts with the thesis — flag the conflict instead.

## Scope

### 1. Standing out — differentiation
Gentle Path's current constraints (no gamification, no streaks, no social feed, no AI companion, narrow single-action focus) are deliberate product decisions, not gaps waiting to be filled. Research whether and how other apps — in recovery, sobriety tracking, habit/behavior change, and therapy-adjacent categories — have won by being narrower or calmer than their competitors, rather than by adding more. Where a genuine market opportunity exists in what Gentle Path already refuses to do, say so and explain why. Where the constraint is just a limitation with no upside, say that too.

### 2. Marketing right
What's actually working for apps in this category right now — organic and paid. Specifically: what's working on TikTok for wellness/recovery-adjacent apps (creator-driven, founder-driven, or neither), App Store Optimization basics for this category (what keywords/categories competitors rank under, how they position their listing), and any examples of a recovery-adjacent app that grew primarily through content rather than ads. Suraj already has access to Decodo (Reddit research), TikTok Creator Marketplace, Modash, and Collabstr from prior work — use them where relevant, especially Decodo for actual recovery-community conversations (Reddit) about what people in this space wish existed or complain about in apps they've tried.

### 3. Very good all around — the execution bar
Pick 5–8 real, currently-live, well-regarded apps in adjacent categories. For each: what do they specifically do well at the craft level — onboarding flow, interaction polish, pricing/monetization model, retention mechanics — and would copying it help Gentle Path or fight its thesis? "Well-reviewed" and "worth copying" are not the same thing; say which is which.

## Out of scope

- Don't propose specific features or UI to build. Surface patterns and name the opportunity or risk; the build decision happens separately.
- Don't recommend anything requiring backend, auth, payments, or live AI — those are explicitly deferred per `AGENTS.md`.
- Don't recommend streak mechanics, badge/achievement gamification, public social feeds, or anything that trades "action over soothing" for engagement-maximizing design. These aren't a style preference to weigh against others — they contradict the product thesis directly. If something in this category shows up as genuinely effective for competitors, report it under "explicitly rejected patterns" below, not as a recommendation.

## Guardrails

- Every factual claim — download numbers, ratings, pricing, "this app has feature X" — needs a real, checkable source: the actual App Store listing, an actual review, a dated article. If you can't verify something, write "unverified" plainly rather than stating it as fact.
- Don't infer a competitor has a feature because apps like it usually do. Check the actual app or listing.
- "Most top apps in this category do X" is an observation, not a recommendation. Explain why X works for them and whether it's compatible with this specific thesis — frequency across competitors isn't itself an argument.
- Distinguish popularity from quality. Some elite-tier apps win on distribution, timing, or budget rather than on anything transferable to a solo-built app at this stage — note where that's likely the case.

## Return format

Four sections, matching the three scope areas above plus one more:

1. **Standing out** — differentiation opportunities and honest gaps, each with reasoning.
2. **Marketing right** — sourced findings on what's working for distribution in this category, TikTok specifically called out.
3. **Execution bar** — the 5–8 apps, what each does well, and whether it transfers.
4. **Explicitly rejected patterns** — things that are common in the category but incompatible with the thesis, one line each on why, so it's clear they were considered and ruled out rather than missed.

Every claim carries its source. Anything uncertain is flagged as uncertain, not smoothed into a confident-sounding sentence. This gets reviewed by Claude and Suraj before anything in it becomes a decision — that review step is not optional, and it works better the less polishing-over-gaps there is to catch.
